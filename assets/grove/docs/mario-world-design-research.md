# Nintendo world-design research translated for the Grove

This note records high-level design principles from Nintendo's own public material. The Grove applies the principles through original architecture, interactions, names, assets, and layouts; it does not reproduce Mario characters, level geometry, visual branding, collectibles, or sound.

## Primary-source findings

- **One delight per playable area.** The *Super Mario Bros. Wonder* team described aiming for at least one element in every course that would surprise or delight players, supported by rapid cross-discipline prototyping.
- **Function before form.** In the *Super Mario 3D World* developer interview, mechanics were established before the cat visual identity was chosen. The Grove should begin with what a landmark lets the player do.
- **Dense but readable play.** *Super Mario 3D World* combines high idea density with a camera designed to reduce the chance of getting lost. Grove zones should be compact, landmark-led, and readable without rotating the camera.
- **Secrets advertise themselves gently.** Nintendo's official *Super Mario Odyssey* material points players toward sparkles, unusual groups, residents, seeds, and regional cues. Grove discoveries should be visible enough to invite curiosity without becoming compulsory objectives.
- **Distinct places need distinct identities.** *Odyssey* uses strongly differentiated kingdoms, local activities, shops, souvenirs, and regional currencies. Grove uses distinct shop/park silhouettes, materials, activities, and ambient stories while keeping one shared economy.
- **Animation communicates state.** The *Wonder* team describes animation as a way to communicate what a character is doing or feeling. Grove landmarks and interactables should visibly change state when activated.
- **Social presence should help, not punish.** *Wonder*'s “casual connection” was designed so other players can assist or greet but cannot attack or obstruct. Grove's park remains fully enjoyable solo and treats nearby players as optional ambience and support.

## Grove translation

1. Keep the mall atrium as the indoor navigation anchor and the Bloom Chimes as the park anchor.
2. Use one compact park loop with short optional deviations, not a maze.
3. Place three coloured petal markers where the production camera can glimpse them from the loop.
4. Let the three petals awaken the Bloom Chimes through light, scale, particles, and sound; completion is delightful but never required.
5. Preserve quiet seating, broad paths, no failure state, no time pressure, and no competitive reward.
6. Give later outdoor zones their own single interaction thesis rather than repeating the petal hunt.
